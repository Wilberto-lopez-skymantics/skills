# Composition references: Example Ledger, free trial

This is a made-up example, for the page in `page-brief.example.md`. The things described, where
they were seen and the days are invented to show the form. In real work every note is of one thing
that was looked at. The form and its rules are in `references/composition.md`.

This file is working material. It is not part of the page brief and is not handed to anyone who
reviews the page.

## R1  The reminder letters a water company sends

- Kind: own-world
- Where: Three letters the owner kept, from one unpaid bill.
- Seen: looked at 2026-11-06
- Does: Each letter is one date in large figures and one sentence of what happens on it, and
  nothing else: under 40 words. Read in a row, the three dates are the whole story: sent, reminded,
  paid. No pictures at all.
- Take: Dated lines as the picture. A promise about time shown as a short row of dates. One thing
  said at length, date by date, in place of several things said briefly.
- Leave: The company's letterhead and its wording.

## R2  The back page of a seed catalogue

- Kind: other-craft
- Where: A printed mail-order catalogue, spring edition, the owner's copy.
- Seen: looked at 2026-11-06
- Does: One promise across the top in large type. Under it a table, not a picture: each row a
  variety, with a price and one line of what it is good for. The order form is the bottom third of
  the same page, already half filled in as an example.
- Take: A table as the picture. The thing to do shown already begun.
- Leave: Its drawings and its wording.

## R3  The page of a van-hire firm

- Kind: other-page
- Where: The firm's public booking page.
- Seen: looked at 2026-11-07
- Does: The first window is the headline and a price, with no picture. Below it, one section far
  longer than the rest: a day's hire followed hour by hour, with what it costs at each point. The
  questions are all open and read as a short letter. About 300 words in all.
- Take: One section that leads and carries the argument. Questions left open.
- Leave: Its photographs of vans, and its wording.

## R4  The owner's own invoice template

- Kind: owner
- Where: The document the owner used before the product existed.
- Seen: looked at 2026-11-06
- Does: One narrow column. Every line of work is numbered in the left margin. The total is the
  largest thing on the page, bottom right, with the due date beside it.
- Take: A narrow column. The figure that matters set largest, with its date beside it.
- Leave: The owner's letterhead, and the numbers in the margin: on an invoice they are referred to,
  and on a page they would number sections whose order tells a visitor nothing.

## R5  The pages of three invoicing services

- Kind: same-category
- Where: Their public pages for a free trial.
- Seen: looked at 2026-11-07
- Does: All three: a headline, a line and a button, centred, over a screenshot of the invoice
  editor; then a section for each question, all about the same size: three cards with an icon each;
  three numbered steps; a strip of logos; a folded list of questions. Between 600 and 800 words.
  With the names covered they cannot be told apart.
- Take: Nothing of the form. What a visitor arriving from a search has just seen twice.
- Leave: The form, which is the one to stand apart from.

## Compositions proposed

| Decision | A. The dates | B. The ledger page | C. The common form |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starts from | R1, R3 | R2, R4 | R5 |
| 1. Skeleton | The schedule leads: one long section takes it apart date by date and answers what changes and how it works together. Then the proof, the objections, the action. 350 words | The first window, and under it one table that is the rest of the page: what it does, what it costs, what happens at 30 days. Then the action. 180 words | A section for each question, all about the same size: problem, benefits, steps, proof, questions, action. 650 words |
| 2. First window | Words beside the picture. The action is a button | Words above, the picture below and running off the window. The action is the email field itself | Words centred over the picture. The action is a button |
| 3. The picture | The reminders schedule: three dates | The invoice editor on a desktop | The invoice editor on a desktop |
| 4. First proof | The figure of 6 days, under the action | The figure of 6 days, beside the headline | The figure of 4,200 accounts, in a line across the foot of the window |
| 5. Benefits | In the schedule section: under each date, what the visitor no longer does | Rows of the table | A row of equal parts, a heading and a sentence each |
| 6. How it works | In the same section: the editor on a phone, shown once beside the first date | One line under the picture | Three numbered steps in a row |
| 7. Proof | One quotation, large, with the figure beside it | The figure, large, with its source | The quotation in a card |
| 8. Questions | The four objections, all open | The last rows of the table | A folded list |
| 9. Rhythm | One narrow column. The schedule section wider and far longer than the rest | One narrow column throughout. The table is the page | Full-width sections of equal height on alternating grounds |
| 10. Recurs | The dates, set the same way wherever they return | Nothing | Nothing |

What each gives up:

- **A** shows no picture of the product until the visitor scrolls, and has no section of benefits.
  It asks the schedule to stand for the product, which works only if the headline is about being
  paid on time and the dates' own headings say what changes.
- **B** says least. A visitor with a doubt the table does not answer has nowhere else to look, so
  it suits the readers of the newsletter, who know the offer, better than those from the
  advertisement. On a phone its picture has to be cut high so that the action stays in the window.
- **C** is the common form: the page this visitor has just seen twice. It is the safest to read and
  the hardest to remember, and at 650 words it says each thing more than once.

A differs from B and from C in every row. B differs from C in every row but 3 and 10: both show
the editor, and neither has anything that recurs.

None of the three rests on a tell. C is the common skeleton without the cards and icons of the
pages in R5, which it does not need. Its steps are numbered because they are a sequence.

## Chosen

A, by the owner, on 2026-11-10. It is D-003 in the brief.
