# Pictures

A picture on a landing page is evidence or it is ground. As evidence it shows the thing that is
offered. As ground it is something the brand has chosen to stand on. Either way it is real, the
page may use it, and the brief knows where it came from.

The rules that were taken from the third source are in
`sources/taste-skill/skills/taste-skill/references/photos.md`. That file is written for a skill with
a photograph library and a script of its own. Neither is here, so read it for its rules and pass
over its commands (`references/ownership.md` says which parts stand). This file says how those
rules are used in this skill, and adds what that file leaves out.

## Where a picture comes from

In this order.

1. **The owner's own.** A photograph of the product, the people or the place, a screen of the
   product, a drawing the brand already owns.
2. **A picture found elsewhere, under a licence that allows it.** The public domain, CC0, CC BY or
   CC BY-SA, from a collection that states the licence for each file: a museum's open collection,
   Wikimedia Commons. Look at it at full size before it is offered. It is sharp, it shows what the
   page says it shows, and it has no name, sign or mark of another business in it and no face that
   would be taken for the owner's staff or customers.
3. **None.** The product at work on sample material, a figure the brief can prove, or type alone.

Never a generated picture, a composite, or a mood photograph standing where the subject should be
(a sky, a forest, hands on a laptop). The third source's own list is under "Never" in its file.

Each picture is entered in the brief under Proof before it is used: what it is, where it is from,
who made it, and its licence. Proof is a closed list, and that holds for pictures as for prices.
A file is saved into the project only once the owner has agreed to the picture. A draft shown
before that may load it from the collection that publishes it, and the report says that it does.

## A picture that is not the owner's

It is never passed off as theirs. A photograph of another bakery does not stand for this bakery,
and a painting is credited as the painting it is. Where a picture stands in for one the owner has
still to supply, the page says so beside it and the report lists it, as with any sample material
(`references/ownership.md`, "Facts").

Credits are one line in the footer, with the full list behind it: for each picture its maker, its
title where it has one, the collection, the licence and a link to the source. A licence that asks
for more (CC BY-SA asks that a changed copy carry the same licence) is followed.

## Words on a picture

A picture carries words only where it is calm and empty, at the widest size and at the phone's
crop. Otherwise the words go in a solid box or off the picture. Nothing is laid over a picture to
make room for words: no tint, no gradient, no fade, no blur. A picture that needs one is the wrong
picture, or the wrong part of the right one.

Then measure it. Contrast against a picture is not one number, because the ground changes under
every letter.

- Take the page with the words hidden, at each size, and read the pixels where the words sit.
- Hold the colour of the words against the darkest twentieth of those pixels if the words are
  dark, or the lightest twentieth if they are light.
- Small text needs 4.5 to 1 there. A large headline needs 3 to 1.
- Say in the report what was measured and at which sizes.

The picture gets a crop of its own for the phone, with the subject and the empty part both inside
it. And it gets a ground colour taken from itself, so that where it has not loaded the words still
read.

## Showing it

- It is there from the first paint: never faded in, never hidden until a script runs.
- Its width and height are set, so that nothing jumps when it arrives.
- No effect is put on it: no duotone, grain, halftone or blend. One light and one grade across the
  set, and no frame twice on a page.
- Its text alternative says what is in it, in the page's own words.
