# References

This is a made-up example. The things described, where they were seen and the days are invented to
show the form of a note. In a real study every note is of one thing that was looked at.

## R1  Stamped metal asset tags on valves and hydrants

- Kind: own-world
- Where: On the utility's own assets. The owner photographed twelve at the North depot; the
  photographs are theirs and are not kept in this folder.
- Seen: looked at 2026-11-20
- Does: Condensed capitals and figures punched into bare metal at one size, widely spaced. The asset
  number is the largest thing on the tag. No colour but the metal and, on hydrants, one band of
  signal paint. Everything sits inside a ruled box with square corners.
- Take: Condensed capitals for names and labels. The code as the most prominent thing in a row. Ruled
  boxes with square corners. One signal colour, kept for the thing to act on.
- Leave: The utility's crest and the maker's name, which are theirs.

## R2  Title blocks of the utility's as-built drawings

- Kind: own-world
- Where: Sheets from the drawing office, 1980s, seen at the depot.
- Seen: looked at 2026-11-20
- Does: A grid of ruled cells in the corner of every sheet, each with a small capital label above a
  larger entry. Two weights of line. Figures of equal width for dimensions and dates. Black on paper
  that has gone slightly blue-grey.
- Take: A small label over a larger value, in a ruled cell. Two weights of line and no fills.
  Figures of equal width.
- Leave: The drawing office's stamp and its sheet numbering.

## R3  A printed regional bus timetable

- Kind: other-craft
- Where: The county's printed timetable booklet, current edition.
- Seen: looked at 2026-11-19
- Does: Dense tables of times in a humanist sans with strong, open figures. On the cover, route
  numbers are reversed out of rounded lozenges in one bright colour on a dark ground. Inside,
  alternate rows are tinted, not ruled. Notes are keyed by small symbols explained once.
- Take: Figures that read at a glance in a dense table. A tint in place of a line between rows. A
  rounded lozenge for the one thing to find. A dark ground with one bright colour.
- Leave: The operator's livery colours and its route symbols.

## R4  A surveyor's field book

- Kind: other-craft
- Where: A levelling book from the utility's archive.
- Seen: looked at 2026-11-20
- Does: Cream paper, ruled feint in green-blue. Entries in pencil; printed column heads in a small
  old-style serif. Rows tall enough to write in outdoors. No boxes: columns and rows are told apart
  by ruled lines only.
- Take: Warm paper and coloured ruling in place of boxes. A serif for headings. Rows roomy enough
  for a gloved hand.
- Leave: The handwriting and the printer's imprint.

## R5  The telemetry screens of a regional control room

- Kind: other-software
- Where: Seen on a visit to the control room. No pictures were taken.
- Seen: from memory
- Does: Light figures on a dark ground, so that the room can stay dim. A state is a coloured mark
  with a word beside it. One fixed-width face throughout. Nothing moves unless a value changes.
- Take: A dark ground for a screen read in glances. A state as a mark and a word. No movement
  without cause.
- Leave: Its alarm colours, which mean something else there.

## R6  Two maintenance-management products the owner has tried

- Kind: same-category
- Where: Their public sites, and the trial accounts the owner opened.
- Seen: looked at 2026-11-18
- Does: Both use a plain sans, cool greys, a blue accent, soft cards lifted by shadows and a column
  of icons down the left. Their first screens are dashboards of tiles. With the names covered, the
  two cannot be told apart.
- Take: Nothing of the look. What a supervisor already expects of how such a tool behaves.
- Leave: The look, which is the one to stand apart from.

## R7  The whiteboard at the North depot

- Kind: owner
- Where: A photograph the owner sent.
- Seen: looked at 2026-11-18
- Does: The week ruled into days by hand, one row for each crew. Three marker colours, each with one
  meaning the whole depot knows: green for done, red for not done, black for planned. Large capitals.
- Take: Colour kept for the state a thing is in, each colour with one meaning.
- Leave: The handwriting.
